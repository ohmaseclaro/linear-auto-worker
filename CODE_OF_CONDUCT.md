# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Argue about the code, not the person. Assume the other reader is competent and
acting in good faith until they give you a real reason not to.

## What's expected

- **Critique the work.** "This drops the error" is useful. "This is amateur" is not.
- **Say how you know.** Especially here — this project's whole discipline is measuring
  rather than recalling. "I ran X and got Y" ends an argument that opinions cannot.
- **Take a correction well, and give one kindly.** Being wrong in public is the normal cost
  of working in public.
- **Respect the maintainer's time and scope.** A rejected feature is not a personal verdict.
  This is a single-operator tool, and keeping it small is a design decision, not neglect.

## What's not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks, in issues, pull requests, commits, or anywhere
  else connected to this project.
- Demeaning or exclusionary comments about who someone is — their gender, sexuality, race,
  ethnicity, nationality, religion, disability, age, or appearance.
- Sexual attention or imagery.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption: repeatedly reopening settled decisions, or badgering people who have
  said no.

## Scope

Anywhere this project happens — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, code
comments — and to public behaviour that is clearly representing the project.

## Enforcement

Report a problem by opening a private report through
[GitHub's abuse reporting][gh-report], or by contacting the maintainer
([@ohmaseclaro](https://github.com/ohmaseclaro)) directly through GitHub. Reports are
handled privately.

The maintainer may edit or remove comments, close or lock threads, or block accounts, as the
situation warrants. Escalation is proportionate: a note first where a note is enough, a
block where it isn't.

If you are the person being reported, you will be told what the problem is and given a
chance to respond, unless the behaviour is severe enough that removing it first is the only
sensible order of events.

[gh-report]: https://github.com/contact/report-abuse
